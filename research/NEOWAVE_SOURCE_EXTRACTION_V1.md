# NeoWave source extraction — first-pass research note

**Status:** preliminary OCR outline only; not a complete or authoritative rulebook.  
**Primary source:** Glenn Neely, *Mastering Elliott Wave*, Version 2.0, with Eric Hall, © 1990.  
**File reviewed:** English scanned PDF, 169 pages.  
**Method:** OCR of PDF pages 1–16 (incomplete; OCR quality varies). PDF page numbers below refer to the supplied scan, not printed page numbers.

## Why the source matters
The opening material presents the Neely method as a sequential, rule-driven analysis process rather than visual pattern matching. It emphasizes that structure generally takes precedence over time observations, that analysts should wait for patterns to complete before acting, and that all market action should be accounted for rather than forcing a preferred scenario. These are high-level methodological statements, not executable predicates.

## Opening-chapter observations (limited)
- The author describes price behavior as the primary observable and cautions against relying on transformed indicators as a substitute for price.
- The text states that structure should generally supersede time observations and that the greatest predictability is expected near/after pattern completion.
- It calls for meticulous analysis built from smaller-scale structure upward, rather than imposing a long-term count first.
- It explicitly warns against beginning with a preconceived scenario and forcing the market into it.
- The book acknowledges indeterminacy and alternate possibilities; the software must preserve an unknown/ambiguous state rather than fabricate certainty.

These statements are paraphrased from the opening pages and should not be mistaken for the complete technical definitions of the named rules.
